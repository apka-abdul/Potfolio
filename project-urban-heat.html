Urban Heat Island Analysis

Comprehensive thermal analysis of metropolitan areas using Landsat 8 satellite imagery to identify and map urban heat islands for climate adaptation planning.

← Back to Projects

Project Overview

Urban heat islands (UHIs) are metropolitan areas that experience significantly higher temperatures than their surrounding rural areas. This phenomenon is primarily caused by human activities and the modification of land surfaces. This project utilized Landsat 8 thermal infrared data to map and analyze UHI patterns across major metropolitan regions.

The analysis focused on identifying temperature variations, correlating them with land use patterns, and providing actionable insights for urban planning and climate adaptation strategies. The project combined remote sensing techniques with GIS spatial analysis to create comprehensive heat maps and statistical models.

Methodology

Data Acquisition

  • Downloaded Landsat 8 Level-2 surface temperature products from USGS Earth Explorer
  • Selected cloud-free scenes from summer months (June-August) for maximum UHI effect
  • Collected auxiliary data: land use/land cover maps, population density, vegetation indices
  • Gathered meteorological data from local weather stations for validation

Data Processing

  • Atmospheric correction using DOS (Dark Object Subtraction) method
  • Conversion of digital numbers to land surface temperature (LST) using thermal bands
  • Calculation of NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index) for vegetation analysis
  • Geometric correction and co-registration of multi-temporal datasets

Analysis Techniques

  • Statistical analysis of temperature distributions across different land use types
  • Spatial autocorrelation analysis using Moran's I to identify hot spot clusters
  • Regression modeling to correlate LST with NDVI, built-up density, and population
  • Temporal analysis comparing daytime and nighttime temperature patterns

Study Area Coverage

The analysis revealed significant temperature variations across the metropolitan area, with commercial and industrial zones showing the highest surface temperatures. Areas with higher vegetation cover demonstrated a strong cooling effect, with temperatures up to 8.5°C lower than densely built-up areas.

Impact & Applications

The findings from this project have been instrumental in informing urban planning decisions and climate adaptation strategies. Key applications include:

  • Identification of priority areas for urban greening and tree planting initiatives
  • Guidance for cool roof and cool pavement implementation programs
  • Input for urban climate models and heat vulnerability assessments
  • Support for public health interventions during heat wave events
  • Evidence base for sustainable urban development policies

Future Work

Building on this analysis, future research directions include:

  • Integration of high-resolution thermal imagery from aerial surveys
  • Development of predictive models for UHI intensity under different climate scenarios
  • Analysis of the relationship between UHI and energy consumption patterns
  • Creation of real-time monitoring systems using satellite data streams
